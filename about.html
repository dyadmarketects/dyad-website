About me & my journey

Design and data, one system

Vincent Inkosi King is the founder of DYAD Marketects, an Albuquerque, New Mexico branding, web design and content studio. With 20+ years in immersive media, 3D animation, graphic design and digital marketing, he pairs creative work with data-driven strategy. DYAD stands for Duality Yields Agile Development: two forces, one system.

'90

Two worlds, one house

I was born at the end of 1990 and raised by an electrical engineer father and an artist mother who homeschooled us to think critically. Logic and creativity were never separate rooms in that house.

'03

Animating a planetarium dome at 12

At 12, I joined the UNM LodeStar Astronomy production team as a 3D model designer and animator on Arrow to the Sun, a fulldome 360 production for the planetarium at the New Mexico Museum of Natural History and Science. It’s based on Gerald McDermott’s Caldecott Medal winning children’s book, and it grew into a co-venture between McDermott and UNM’s ARTS Lab.

The timing was special. Fulldome theaters had gone from a handful to more than 200 worldwide, with new ones opening almost weekly, and UNM was a leader in exploring what domes could do beyond planetarium shows. Arrow to the Sun was UNM’s first fulldome production, made as the book was released in a 30th anniversary edition. It pairs a Native American tale with a first introduction to the solar system, and the team made a point of developing dome content with members of traditionally underrepresented groups.

The reel below is a 2.5 minute collection of clips from the production while it was in progress, showing the book’s world coming to life on the dome.

Arrow to the Sun fulldome production, work in progress

'04

First of its kind at UNM

At 13, I enrolled as a student and teaching assistant in a college level course most adults had never heard of. In spring 2004, UNM began offering the world’s first full semester course in Full Dome Production.

Students from fine arts, science and engineering backgrounds worked in collaborative teams to create 1 to 3 minute films for an immersive dome. Frames were built as 3200 x 3200 pixel images at 30 frames per second, through a pipeline of specialized software that was barely documented. Almost everyone walked in with one piece of the skill set and quickly hit parts of the process that were completely new. The team approach was the point: pool your skills, split the work, and spend your energy on the story instead of the software.

The class pushed us to think about what makes a dome different. What does it mean when something enters from behind you instead of in front? How does peripheral vision change motion, scale and time? The big thrill came at the end of the semester, when our work played on LodeStar’s 55 foot dome at a public screening. LodeStar, built in 2000 at the New Mexico Museum of Natural History and Science, was only the second digital dome theater of its kind in the world.

Sources
  • Hue Walker, “Inventing a Collaborative Full Dome Production Course,” ARTS Lab, University of New Mexico
'09

Maya Skies, then architecture

I graduated from Albuquerque Academy in 2009 and took my senior project to the UNM ARTS Lab. As the lab’s Advanced Multimedia Assistant, I was part of the team responsible for Tales of the Maya Skies, an educational, immersive 360 degree animated film about ancient Maya astronomy that has been shown around the globe.

ARTS Lab supports experimental artistic research and production at UNM in emerging technologies. It’s an interdisciplinary center connecting art, science, business and technology, with a vision of becoming a key catalyst for the education and research that grows an advanced media industry in New Mexico. It lives in the Digital Garage, a black box space built for performance, experimentation and research in digital graphics, sound and real time immersive projection. The Garage’s major resources include a 15 foot hemispheric dome built in partnership with the LodeStar Astronomy Center, a large motion capture system and a sizable green screen environment.

The ARTS Lab trained me in Maya, Rhino 3D and Final Cut Pro. In my free time I taught myself Photoshop, After Effects and Premiere Pro.

Then architecture

After Maya Skies I studied architecture at UNM. In my second year, an assignment asked us to illustrate the transformation between organic and mechanical, and to make it symmetrical. I said no. Living things aren’t perfectly symmetrical; our hearts sit off to the left. I tried to draw the conversation between organic asymmetry and our idea of fabricated, precise symmetry. Every imperfection in each evolutionary iteration creates a reason to grow.

Tales of the Maya Skies: the Hero Twins, the birth of corn and the creation of the Sun and the Moon

'10

The DYAD within, validated

This is where three of my passions collided: skateboarding, immersive media and science. At the ARTS Lab I worked on fulldome animation, CNC routing and a futuristic skateboard simulation, a 360 degree immersive skateboarding game you ride on a real deck in front of the dome.

I was blessed with the opportunity to be featured in a Discovery Channel Canada episode about that exact mix, which documented our production of the simulation. The work was unveiled at Mesa del Sol in front of the governor and made Albuquerque Business First. The rebel and the nerd, creating in unison.

The lab’s interactive dome work also included HexDex, a six sided motion platform designed by UNM computer science professor Joe Kniss for “the feel and fun of skateboarding without the road rash.” Two HexDexes sit inside a larger rotating platform, and in the asteroids game shifting your weight steers the view.

Sources
  • UNM School of Engineering, “HexDex takes gaming to new dimensions” (2011)
'11 & '12

Giving back

I was Lead Instructor for Summer Majik, a STEM initiative of the UNM Summer Enrichment camp run by the late Debra Johns, a longtime Albuquerque educator. I taught it for two semesters, in 2011 and 2012.

Across an intensive two week program, I taught middle school students production basics with professional software: After Effects animation, Photoshop, GarageBand audio and chroma key compositing. They researched influential African American figures in New Mexico history and animated their stories with original audio and voiceover, then presented them to families and faculty.

'12

Architecture major, film intern

In 2012 I earned a spot in UNM’s intensive IFDM (Interdisciplinary Film and Digital Media) summer internship at the ARTS Lab, even as an architecture major.

The internship produced Chaco Canyon: Earth, Time, People, a fulldome film built from 360 degree HDR photography. That meant shooting bracketed panoramas on location, stitching them so they sit correctly on a dome, then bringing them into After Effects and Autodesk Maya, where a flat photo becomes a space you can move through.

'12 & '13

Recalibrating into marketing

Architecture tickled the fascination but didn’t scratch the itch, so I recalibrated toward marketing.

ExploreMedia360

Working for ExploreMedia, one of my tasks was moderating Google tours for our clients, giving their customers an immersive experience of our clients’ establishments through Google Maps. 360° images were sent to me and I stitched the panoramas to one another so they were oriented correctly, giving the viewer a clear, easily navigable virtual space to explore. By contract, I also scrubbed through every pano and blurred all human faces and license plates. A couple of those clients were DriveTime Used Cars, a nationwide company, and Art Gone Wild Galleries in Santa Fe, NM and Key West, FL.

As ExploreMedia’s graphic designer, I created book covers and multiple iterations of website mockups in Photoshop and After Effects. Some of my clients were Trend Magazine (Santa Fe), Art Gone Wild (Santa Fe), Bon Marche (Santa Fe) and Al’s Upholstery (Albuquerque).

KUNM

I also worked as the Graphic Designer for the KUNM Development Department, designing a monthly electronic newsletter, web buttons and icons, direct mail pieces from response cards to envelopes, advertising specialties, print ads, bumper stickers and more. I’d help with bulk mailings and other clerical and customer service work too.

Designing for a nonprofit taught me to multitask and prioritize, because I had to produce multiple iterations of ads and articles quickly, on tight schedules, to bring in essential donations.

'19

Design grounded by gravity

In 2019 I worked as an Environmental Graphic Designer and Production Artist at Century Sign Builders, a local sign design and manufacturing company.

Every team had three roles. A project manager assessed the customer’s needs, ran site surveys and met with contractors and architects. An environmental graphic designer created visually dynamic, ADA compliant, branded wayfinding based on the customer’s needs and the environment, along with full production specs and installation guides. A production artist took approved designs and formatted them for production.

The unique thing about my team was that it was just me and a project manager, so I had to learn everything quickly, from concept to design to production to installation. When my project manager was out on an extended leave, I worked directly with clients like Utah Navajo Health System, Inc. to take the entire project from start to finish.

I used architectural plans and site visit assessments to create to scale renderings, not only for the client to see, but for our production team to fabricate the signage in house and our installation team to mount it. Every specification had to be spelled out, from quantities, messages and locations to substrates, sizes, hardware and mounting technique. That’s how the client and every department stayed on the same page.

This role was a great combination of data and design. Architecture and engineering. Form and function.

'20

Building marketing at DSI

In 2020, COVID layoffs moved me on from Century Sign Builders, and I dove even further into the digital side of design as a Graphic Designer at DSI, Document Solutions, Inc. Rather than designs grounded by gravity, my designs were now grounded by customer connection, both external and internal: driving sales, supporting technical teams and making internal operations run better.

I grew into Marketing Manager & Senior Graphic Designer, and built and now lead an entire marketing department running campaigns across web, print, social, email, events and video. That includes a full redesign of DSI’s website, rebuilt page by page around customer and employee experience with real team photos and SEO copy, and the March to the Madness lead generation event, where a $400 budget turned into $1.9M in profit.

I was one of the first non-sales employees to receive DSI’s Presidential Trip (President’s Club), earned for revenue-generating work, brand visibility campaigns, a comprehensive website rebrand and embodying DSI’s core values.

'22

Rebel meets professional

I stopped treating the skateboarder and the strategist as different people. I channeled the same resourcefulness into client work, and it started showing.

'23

Classroom to career

I continue to volunteer my time presenting to and mentoring students on transitioning from classroom to career.

eCademy Junior Achievement at DSI, 2023

On April 18, 2023, about 40 students from eCademy High School visited DSI’s Albuquerque headquarters with Junior Achievement. One of the best ways for students to learn about the world of work is to spend time on site with a business, get a feel for a professional workplace and ask questions about a field they’re interested in.

I presented “Net-iquette,” a topic I coined for best practices on social media so you present your best self to potential employers. I also talked about the importance of identifying your passions and finding a job where you can do just that.

Our team ran a series of hands on activities on career research and preparation, job hunting tools, professionalism and ethics in the workplace, and professional action plans. Students were able to:

  • Recognize career clusters and potential jobs that align with their passions
  • Professionally problem solve workplace scenarios
  • Understand why researching the requirements for a position matters
  • Develop job hunting tools like networking, resumes and interviewing skills
  • Identify the next steps in their own career preparation

It was a great opportunity to show Vincent King’s shared value with DSI of Selfless Service.

CNM Advanced Adobe Creative Suite, 2024

In 2024 I presented to Sonia Crawford’s Advanced Adobe Creative Suite class at CNM, sharing my college experience and how to best prepare yourself for a smooth transition from the classroom to a career.

'24

Growing a ranch brand

In 2024 I also partnered with Houser Raspberry Ranch in the Mora Valley as their Marketing Specialist. I lead their PR initiatives, social media content and management, email campaigns and website management.

The branding work covers business cards, menus, documentation, email signatures, flyers, a recruiting initiative, event planning, app development and more, plus comprehensive logo design and brand strategy for their Rusty Nail Saloon. Two years of consistent, on brand social content took the ranch’s profile from 0 to 2.7K followers.

'26

DYAD Marketects is born

Two forces, one system. DYAD Marketects is a studio built on the belief that creativity and analytics were never actually competing, they just needed the right bridge. DYAD stands for Duality Yields Agile Development.

Today I work across DSI, Houser Raspberry Ranch and DYAD Marketects, helping local businesses in Albuquerque and across New Mexico grow.
